# Problem statement

**Do the strange series representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}(1,1))$ interpolate between the
AdS$_2$ and dS$_2$ regions of the DSSYK bulk dual?**

Status: **OPEN** (to be re-verified against the literature as of August 2026; see issue #2).

---

## 1. Background

The double-scaled SYK model (DSSYK) is the limit of the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model in which
$N \to \infty$ and $p \to \infty$ with

$$\lambda = \frac{2p^2}{N} \quad \text{fixed}, \qquad q = e^{-\lambda} \in (0,1).$$

It is exactly solvable by chord-diagram techniques: correlation functions are generating
functions over pairings of Hamiltonian insertions, weighted by $q^{\#\text{crossings}}$. Unlike
the Schwarzian limit of ordinary SYK, DSSYK retains the full energy range, which makes it one of
the very few complete, non-perturbatively solvable models of low-dimensional quantum gravity.

**The identity of its bulk dual is disputed as of 2026.** The main candidates are:

1. **sine-dilaton gravity** — Blommaert, Mertens et al., arXiv:2404.03535, JHEP 06(2025)050;
2. **de Sitter space** (dS$_2$ or dS$_3$) — Narovlansky–Verlinde, Susskind and collaborators;
3. **a $q$-deformed, non-commutative AdS$_2$ quantum homogeneous space** — Berkooz, Isachenkov,
   Narayan, Narovlansky, arXiv:2212.13668 (JHEP 08(2023)076), developed further in
   Schouten–Isachenkov, arXiv:2512.10101.

Line (3) replaces the search for a metric with an algebraic statement: the DSSYK Hamiltonian is
generated by the quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{su}(1,1))$, and the bulk is the corresponding
quantum homogeneous space $\mathrm{AdS}_{2,q}$, a non-commutative deformation of AdS$_2$ whose
deformation parameter $q$ plays the role of a finite gravitational coupling. As $q \to 1$ the
non-commutativity switches off and ordinary AdS$_2$ with the Schwarzian theory is recovered.

## 2. The claim under investigation

The reference paper for this project is

> **Koen Schouten, Mikhail Isachenkov**, *The von Neumann algebraic quantum group
> $\mathrm{SU}_q(1,1) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$ and the DSSYK model*, arXiv:2512.10101
> (v1 December 2025, v2 April 2026), 89 pp.

That paper constructs an operator-algebraic (von Neumann algebraic) quantum group description of
DSSYK: the quantum Gauss decomposition of $\mathrm{SU}_q(1,1) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$, the action of
Casimir operators on quantum homogeneous spaces, and the reduction of the dynamics on
$\mathrm{AdS}_{2,q}$ to DSSYK. The extension of the symmetry group to its normaliser is argued to
be necessary for the quantum group to be consistently defined.

Among its results, the authors find that the regular representation of this quantum group
**contains the "strange series"** — a family of unitary representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}(1,1))$
with **no classical analogue**, existing only for $q \neq 1$ and disappearing in the limit
$q \to 1$.

The authors **argue but do not prove** that the strange series representations *genuinely
interpolate between the AdS and dS regions* of the bulk. Establishing, sharpening, or refuting
this statement is the object of this project. (Issue #4 is responsible for pinning the claim down
to a precise mathematical statement, quoting arXiv:2512.10101 verbatim.)

The same paper explicitly flags that the mathematical status of the relevant Plancherel theory for
the associated quantum semigroup "is not clear to us and likely requires additional assumptions or
structure". Any argument in this project that silently assumes such a Plancherel theory must say so
and open a `gap` issue.

## 3. Why the claim matters

If true, the claim substantially dissolves the bulk-dual dispute: AdS and dS would not be
competing answers but two regions of a single algebraic object, with the strange series as the
bridge between them. Because the strange series exists only at $q \neq 1$, this would further imply
that the de Sitter character of DSSYK is an intrinsically quantum, non-commutative phenomenon,
invisible in the semiclassical limit. That is a strong, falsifiable physical statement.

## 4. Proposed method

Use the Casimir-radial-part technique, applied on the quantum side. The classical, rigorous
version of this machinery is developed in

> **Philip Schlösser, Mikhail Isachenkov**, *Casimir Radial Parts via Matsuki Decomposition*,
> arXiv:2412.19681 (v2 July 2025), 72 pp.

and its use for conformal blocks (Casimir $\to$ radial part $\to$ Calogero–Sutherland /
Pöschl–Teller) in arXiv:1602.01858 (PRL 117, 071602), arXiv:1711.06609, arXiv:1806.09703.

Steps:

1. Set up the strange series representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}(1,1))$ precisely, including the
   extension by the normaliser $\rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$. Conventions in the $q$-deformed literature
   differ; fix them explicitly and once (issue #3).
2. Identify the correct quantum homogeneous space — $\mathrm{AdS}_{2,q}$, the quantum disk, the
   quantum hyperboloid (issue #5).
3. Compute the matrix coefficients of the strange series on that space (issue #6).
4. Extract the **radial part of the Casimir operator**. Expect an explicit $q$-difference
   operator, plausibly of $q$-Pöschl–Teller / Askey–Wilson type (issue #7).
5. Write the effective potential and its parameters as an explicit function of the strange-series
   label, and **identify the quantity whose sign change constitutes "AdS$_2 \to$ dS$_2$"** — the
   sign of the effective curvature, the sign of the exponential term in the Pöschl–Teller
   potential, or the character of the spectrum (continuous vs. discrete, normalisable vs.
   delta-normalisable). Determine whether the strange series is precisely the family that crosses
   it, and where (issue #8).

Deviation from this method is permitted where justified; the justification must be recorded.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

A result is accepted only if all of the following hold.

- **Explicit closed-form formulas**, not prose descriptions of formulas. Where no closed form is
  obtained, this is stated plainly along with whatever partial form is available.
- **The $q \to 1$ limit reproduces known results**: Liouville / Schwarzian wavefunctions, the
  Bessel functions $K_{2is}(e^{-\ell/2})$, and the principal series returning to ordinary AdS$_2$
  with the known Schwarzian density of states.
- **The strange series disappears as $q \to 1$.** It has no classical analogue. This is a sharp
  internal consistency check: a derivation in which it survives at $q = 1$ is wrong.
- **Numerical verification.** Difference equations solved numerically, spectra checked,
  orthogonality / Plancherel measure checked where known, the $q \to 1$ limit checked numerically
  as well as symbolically. Code and output committed. A symbolic result that was not numerically
  tested does not count as verified.
- **Cross-checks against independent literature**: Askey–Wilson technology (arXiv:2605.13956,
  *q-Askey deformations of Double-Scaled SYK*), the sine-dilaton picture (arXiv:2404.03535,
  JHEP 06(2025)050), the de Sitter holography claims, the von Neumann algebra results
  (Jiuci Xu, arXiv:2403.09021 — type II$_1$ factor; Sergio E. Aguilar-Gutierrez,
  arXiv:2511.03779, JHEP 05(2026)080 — *Cosmological Entanglement Entropy from the von
  Neumann Algebra of Double-Scaled SYK & Its Connection with Krylov Complexity*), and van
  der Heijden–Verlinde–Xu, *Quantum Symmetry and Geometry in Double-Scaled SYK*,
  arXiv:2511.08743, JHEP 05(2026)148. (Attribution of 2511.03779 corrected and both titles
  verified against the arXiv records on 2026-08-11, issue #19; see also lit/README.md.)

## 6. Admissible outcomes

In decreasing order of value; all are reportable results and none is a failure except the last.

1. A derivation of the claim.
2. An explicit computation of the radial part together with a precise, falsifiable conjecture
   locating the AdS/dS boundary.
3. A well-argued **negative** result: the strange series does not do what is claimed, with a
   counterexample or an explicit obstruction.
4. A literature map plus a sharpened problem statement identifying exactly what is missing.
5. *(Not acceptable)* A smooth narrative that hides a gap.

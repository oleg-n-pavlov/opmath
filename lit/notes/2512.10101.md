# arXiv:2512.10101 — Schouten–Isachenkov, SU_q(1,1)⋊Z₂ and DSSYK

LaTeX source from arXiv (e-print, retrieved 2026-08-11). Date read: 2026-08-11.

## 2. What it claims

1. A von Neumann algebraic (Kustermans–Vaes locally compact) quantum group treatment of
   DSSYK: the quantum group is SU_q(1,1)⋊Z₂ (Koelink–Kustermans), whose extension by the
   normaliser Z₂ is necessary (Woronowicz no-go for plain SU_q(1,1)) [§3.1–3.2].
2. A quantum Gauss decomposition of this quantum group and the Casimir action in it —
   an Askey–Wilson-type second-order q-difference operator [(3.85)–(3.88)].
3. Reduction of the dynamics on the chord subsector (their quantum AdS_{2,q}) to DSSYK:
   chord states (4.5), transfer matrix T = 2Ω̃/√(1-q²), partition function (4.12) and n-point
   functions (4.16) reproducing Berkooz et al.; length positivity derived, not imposed [§4.1–4.2].
4. **The strange-series claim (the claim under test).** The regular representation contains the
   strange series (via GKK arXiv:0905.2830). Their bilocal operators are (4.18)
   S^ℓ(τ) = e^{τT}(2q ρ̂_st)^{-ℓ-iπ/(2 ln q)}e^{-τT}. Two-point overlaps: at both arguments near
   the lower (Schwarzian/AdS) edge the overlap vanishes as q→1 (4.19); with one argument at the
   lower and one at the upper edge it tends to the Schwarzian 3j-symbol Γ(ℓ±ik₁±ik₂)/Γ(2ℓ)
   (4.20). Conditional on the dS-JT identification of the upper edge (arXiv:2411.16922,
   arXiv:2505.08116), the strange series therefore has non-zero amplitude between AdS and dS
   regions.

## 3. What it actually proves (separated from what it asserts/argues)

**Proved or complete derivations (within the paper's stated framework):**
- The operator-algebraic constructions of §3 (built on Koelink–Kustermans and GKK, which are
  rigorous published mathematics).
- The chord-sector reduction computations of §4.1–4.2 (partition function, discrete-series
  two-point function (4.16)) — derivations reproducing known DSSYK results.
- (4.19)–(4.20) are short computations from (4.15) via the substitution q^{2ℓ} → -q^{2ℓ}; we
  re-derived both (see tex/main.tex §1: they follow from the q-Mehler kernel with t = -q^{2ℓ}
  and an exact reflection identity), modulo two suspected typos listed in §7 below.

**Argued, assumed, or left open (their own words in §6 below):**
- The dS identification of the upper spectral edge is an external proposal, and the claim is
  explicitly conditional on it [(§4.3 end)].
- The physical interpretation of the non-zero AdS↔dS amplitude: "currently unknown"
  [Discussion item 1].
- "In some sense interpolates" [Discussion item 1] — no geometric interpolation statement is
  formulated or proved; the spectral-triple work that "should shed light" on it is cited as
  work in progress [Discussion, spectral-triples item].
- The matrix elements for the classical normaliser SL(2,R)⋊Z₂ were not computed; the classical
  statements are inferred from the q→1 limit of the quantum ones [§2.3 footnote; see §6].
- Plancherel theory for the semigroup alternative (relevant to rival approaches, and flagged by
  PROBLEM.md): status "not clear to us" [§2.3 footnote; see §6].
- Whether S^ℓ is an operator of the physical theory (affiliated to the relevant algebra, with a
  DSSYK chord interpretation) is not discussed. We note (tex §1) that its chord-basis weight is
  (-1)^k q^{2kℓ}, i.e. alternating-sign matter weights; boundedness on the chord sector follows
  from |(-1)^k q^{2kℓ}| = q^{2kℓ} exactly as for O^ℓ.

## 6. Verbatim quotes (locations from v2 source; emphasis original)

Quoted exactly where the paper supports or hedges the claim under test.

1. **Abstract:** "Lastly, we make remarks on the correlation function related to the strange
   series representation, which is argued to interpolate between the AdS and dS regions of our
   $q$-homogeneous space."
2. **§1 (Introduction, outline item for Section 4):** "Additionally, it was shown in
   \[GKK2010\] that the strange series appear in the regular representations of the quantum
   group. In Section 4.3, we discuss the 2-point functions for these representations, and in
   particular argue that these interpolate between two-dimensional Anti-de Sitter and de Sitter
   space."
3. **§3.3 (end):** "Lastly, it was shown in \[GKK2010\] that the left regular representation
   decomposes into a direct integral of the principal unitary series, the discrete series and
   the strange series. \[...\] The appearance of the strange series can be thought of as an
   additional novel, inherently quantum, effect."
4. **§4.3 ("Strange matter"), opening:** "a new, non-classical type of irreducible
   representations that appear in the $q$-deformed Plancherel theorem are the *strange series*.
   Their appearance can be explained by the fact that for every eigenstate of the Casimir
   operator $\tilde\Omega$ with positive eigenvalue, there also is an eigenstate with negative
   eigenvalue \[GKK2010\], whereby the strange series correspond to the 'negative-energy
   counterparts' of the discrete series. The matrix elements of the strange series can be
   obtained by simply replacing $\ell \to \ell + \frac{i\pi}{2\ln q}$ \[BK1993\]."
5. **§4.3, after (4.19):** "This vanishing of the two-point function in this limit is, of
   course, expected as the strange series does not have a classical analogue."
6. **§4.3, closing (after (4.20)):** "such that the strange series has a non-zero amplitude
   between the lower part and upper part of the spectrum . It was proposed in \[BLMPP2025\] and
   \[O2025\] that zooming into the upper part of the spectrum, i.e. $\theta = |\ln(q^2)|k$,
   corresponds to de Sitter JT-gravity. **If this proposal were proven to be correct, the
   strange series would therefore have a non-zero amplitude between the anti-de Sitter and de
   Sitter regions.**" — with the footnote: "Notice that different identifications with de Sitter
   space have been proposed up to date, in particular, by Susskind et al. \[...\] and by
   Verlinde et al. \[...\] The latter proposes that de Sitter space is instead identified with an
   expansion around $E(\theta) = 0$, or $\theta = \pi/2$."
7. **§5 (Discussion), item 1:** "Moreover, supposing that dS space corresponds to the upper part
   of the spectrum, it was shown in Section 4.3 that the strange series gives non-zero
   amplitudes between AdS and dS regions, and thus in some sense interpolates between them. It
   is currently unknown what the correct physics interpretation of such an amplitude would be."
8. **§5 (Discussion), spectral-triples item:** "the natural metric structure provided by a
   spectral triple should shed light on the intriguing overlap of correlation functions between
   AdS and dS regions of the $q$-deformed homogeneous space, which we observed in Section 4.3.
   \[...\] Implementing and exploring this vision in the present context is a work in progress
   \[WPJort\]."
9. **§2.3, footnote (classical normaliser gap):** "We remark here that we have not explicitly
   calculated the matrix elements for the normaliser of the classical group
   $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, as, for the purposes of this paper, we are mostly interested in
   the quantum group case (from which the corresponding $q\to 1$ limit can then be taken). An
   explicit derivation based on the harmonic analysis of the classical group
   $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$, circumventing the reference to the quantum
   group case, therefore still has to be done."
10. **§2.3, footnote (Plancherel caveat for the semigroup alternative):** "Of course, it is
    unclear whether a notion of a Plancherel measure for the semigroup even exists; \[...\] so
    that the current mathematical status of such conjectural Plancherel theory is not clear to
    us and likely requires additional assumptions or structure."

## 7. Suspected typos in v2 (do not affect the mechanism; flagged for #9/#10 verification)

1. **(4.19) exponent:** the Γ_{q²} argument is written with shift `+iπ/ln q`; consistency with
   the substitution ℓ → ℓ + iπ/(2 ln q) (§4.3 text and (4.18)) and with Γ_{q²}(z) built on
   (q²)^z = q^{2z} requires `+iπ/(2 ln q)`. The vanishing conclusion is unaffected: the true
   mechanism is that the same-edge strange denominator is (-q^{2(ℓ±ik₁±ik₂)}; q²)_∞ → ∞
   (each factor → 2), so the normalisation-free ratio to the discrete kernel → 0.
2. **(4.19) middle expression:** with the modulus-preserving *full*-period shift iπ/ln q the
   ratio of q-gamma functions would not tend to 0; with the half-period shift it does. Same
   resolution as 1.
3. (4.20) as printed is confirmed by our derivation (exact reflection identity, tex §1):
   the two e^{iπ} factors — one from the strange substitution, one from θ₁ ≈ π — cancel.
